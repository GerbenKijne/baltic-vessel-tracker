Features

Everything a harbor watcher actually needs

Live map, clustered

MapLibre-rendered positions, colour-coded by data freshness, with native clustering that breaks apart as you zoom in on a harbor.

Unlimited watchlists

Group vessels by route, port, or interest — no per-account cap, with table and card views, plus CSV/GeoJSON import and export.

Geofence & alerts

Circle or polygon geofences, speed thresholds, and stale-signal rules — fire on entry, exit, or a threshold cross, with per-rule cooldowns.

History & track playback

Query any vessel ever seen over a 24h/7d/custom window — a real drawn track with gaps left honest, never bridged into a false straight line.

Pluggable ingestion

Ships with an AISStream adapter and a traffic simulator for trying it out with zero setup; the adapter interface is small enough to add your own.

Runs anywhere Docker does

One Compose file — a NAS, a $5 VPS, or your own machine. PostgreSQL/PostGIS and Redis, no managed cloud service required.

Why self-hosted

Your fleet, your server, your rules

Free tiers on commercial AIS platforms exist to sell you the paid tier — a hard cap on tracked vessels, history windows measured in hours, and a login that answers to someone else's terms of service. None of that is a technical limitation of AIS data itself; it's a business model.

Baltic Vessel Tracker has no accounts to sell you, because it has no business model — it's AGPL-3.0 licensed, so it stays that way for anyone who runs or forks it. You bring the AIS feed (a free AISStream.io key covers most of the Baltic) and the hardware; the app does the parsing, dedup, storage, and the map.

  • No vessel-count limit, ever
  • History retention you set yourself
  • Runs fully offline from any third-party API except your AIS feed
  • Every line of the ingestion pipeline is inspectable
  • No telemetry phoning home
  • Fork it, rebrand it, point it at a different sea entirely
